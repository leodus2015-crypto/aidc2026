## 01  Cover  ·  about 1.5 minutes spoken

**On screen:** *AI Data Centers — From national strategy to secure, scalable AI capability*

Good morning. Thank you for the time.

The title of this briefing is AI Data Centers. Please do not hear that as another conversation about a building. For the next thirty minutes, “data center” means something larger: **how a nation or a leading enterprise turns an AI strategy into capability it can operate, scale, and control.**

The subtitle is the brief in one line: from national strategy to secure, scalable AI capability. The footer tells you who it is for — a practical path for governments and leading enterprises. This is not a technology exhibition, and it is not a vendor shortlist.

We will walk eleven slides on one argument. First, why now: AI took more than sixty years to reach this scale. Then, how far demand has already gone, and how leading economies are turning policy into capital, compute, and power. Then the risk: what happens if critical capability sits in a single external model. Then owning AI: private data, process and experience settle in the agent, and the agent calls models. Then the structure: a sovereign AI factory, four connected layers, and Token → Card → Power as the method that sizes the factory. We close on a decision. Sovereign AI is not a model you rent. It is a factory you own. The AI data center is the foundation.

If you take only one sentence from the cover, take this one: **a strategy without a foundation is still a plan.**

We start with time.

## 02  A long research cycle  ·  about 2.5 minutes spoken

**On screen:** *AI’s long research cycle and rapid adoption*  
**Close:** *66 years*

Look at the timeline. Many people date AI from 2022. The story on this page is longer.

In 1956, the Dartmouth workshop named the field. For decades after that, AI lived in laboratories and in a few industry systems. In the 1980s, expert systems brought fixed rules into business. They worked, until the world changed. The rules were written by hand. The systems were brittle.

1997 is the public memory. Deep Blue beat the world chess champion. A computer appeared to think. It was still a machine built for one task, not a general service.

Compute changed the path. In 2012, AlexNet showed that GPUs could take deep learning to scale. Image recognition left the lab. In 2017, the Transformer rebuilt language AI around attention. Almost every large model since then stands on that paper. In 2022, ChatGPT put generative AI in ordinary hands. AI became a consumer product. By 2025, models are connecting to tools and data. They do not only answer. They start to get work done.

Look at the number on the close bar. **Sixty-six years.** From 1956 to 2022 we passed expert systems, special-purpose machines, deep learning, and the Transformer. Today’s acceleration is not a sudden idea. It is data, stronger computing, and better model design, accumulated until AI became practical at global scale.

For decision-makers, that has two meanings. First, the window looks short, but the foundation is deep. Acting now is standing on a long cycle, not betting on a slogan. Second, the capital, compute, and power on the next slides are what appear once that cycle is finally usable.

## 03  China demand  ·  about 3.5 minutes spoken

**On screen:** *China’s AI demand is scaling across life, work and R&D*  
**Close:** *AI at scale*

Demand is no longer a pilot. This page uses China because life, work, and R&D have all reached industrial scale at the same time. Another country will look different. The logic of the magnitude will not.

Start on the left. The axis is daily token calls, in trillions. Early 2025: about 7 trillion. Mid-year: 30 trillion. Year-end: 100 trillion. March 2026: more than 140 trillion. The page says **about twenty times in about fifteen months.** China recorded about 21.1 quadrillion token calls in 2025. And in that year, inference data volume exceeded training data volume for the first time. Hold on “first time.” The industry story used to be who was training the larger model. Everyday inference is now heavier than training. AI has moved from building a few large models to being used every day.

The three cases on the right show where that use lives.

First, life. Doubao. People ask by voice, learn, search, plan trips, and create images. June 2026: 382 million active users, 54.8 uses per user per month. That is not a tool you open now and then. It is a high-frequency entry point. Every question and every image is an inference call.

Second, work. WorkBuddy. One brief becomes research, a competitor matrix, and an executive report. More than 30 million installs. In the product example, a topic becomes a report in about fifteen minutes. The path on the page is Brief → Research → Analysis → Word + PPT. Office AI is no longer handing you an outline. It is delivering finished work. Finished work means longer context, more tool use, and denser tokens.

Third, R&D. DeepSeek V4. It reads a large codebase, edits files, uses tools, and runs tests. Context window: one million tokens. Official Terminal-Bench 2.1 score: 82.7. The path is Issue → Repository → Code → Test. Once research and engineering work this way, the calls are not short chat turns. They are continuous work against a repository.

Three lines at once: people, offices, engineers. The close bar says AI at scale. Daily users, office agents, and coding agents now create **continuous** demand for compute. Scalable AI infrastructure has become a strategic requirement, not an optional line on an IT list.

One sentence must be said in the room, and the page already says it: **token volume measures AI usage, not power demand.** Compute per token varies by model and task. This slide proves that demand is here. It does not tell you how many megawatts to build. Megawatts come from the method on slide 8.

## 06  Owning AI  ·  about 2 minutes spoken

**On screen:** *The Shift From Using AI to Owning AI*  
**Close:** *Own it*

The last slide said that interaction with a public-cloud model is absorbed. This slide is the other move.

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella’s point is that a company should eventually build AI on its own data, processes and experience, rather than only subscribe to a general model. A general assistant is strong, and it does not know how your business runs. The advantage that is hard to copy is the business knowledge inside it, and that advantage compounds.

Look at the figure. Across the top of the left frame are four applications: operations, customer service, decision making and innovation. Knowledge and enterprise data enter the agent from either side. The agent orchestrates, plans and acts. General-purpose computing and storage are the base. The right frame is not a handover of that material into someone else’s training set. The agent calls models through a gateway: a general model, a domain model and a custom model. AI computing sits underneath those models.

The close is **Own it.** Your data, your process, your experience. Private data, process and experience enter the agent and stay there.

The next slide puts this inside a national and enterprise control boundary.

## 07  Sovereignty  ·  about 3 minutes spoken

**On screen:** *A national AI factory builds capability under local control*  
**Close:** *Local control*

Start with the picture on the left. The oval is the national control boundary. In the center is the national AI factory: a secure compute and model platform. Beside it is the strategic data you must govern — government, citizens, industry. The factory is not sealed. It connects to global models and technology through a controlled gateway. Inside the boundary it serves public services and industry, and it grows a local ecosystem through schools, universities, and the public.

That is what we mean by sovereignty. Not a closed country. **National capability under local control, while staying connected to global innovation.**

The four reasons on the right are why it matters. Walk them 01 to 04.

01. Protect strategic data. Who can access it, where it is processed, and how it is used must be set by local policy. If data leaves by default, or enters someone else’s training flow by default, no amount of compliance at the application layer will buy the control back.

02. Serve local priorities. Models have to fit local languages, laws, and the real work of public services and industry. A global model can be strong and still fail to work in your regulations, your sector, or your documents.

03. Build skills and shared access. Schools, universities, startups, and the public need tools and a way to learn. Otherwise you can build the factory and still have an empty ecosystem, with talent growing on someone else’s platform.

04. Keep choice and continuity. Use global models and technology. Keep operating control at home, and keep a fallback for critical services. That is the line on the page.

The close is Local control. Keep sensitive data in approved environments. External connections follow local policy. Only with a factory can you stay connected to the world and still keep the roots at home. Next we split the factory into four layers, and we see which layer will choke everything above it.

## 08  Four connected layers  ·  about 2.5 minutes spoken

**On screen:** *A four-layer AI DC architecture underpins sovereign AI strategy*  
**Close:** *Stable, extensible* — an AI DC needs a stable architecture that scales flexibly and keeps pace with rapid change in foundation models.

Read from the bottom up. The cutaway on the right is the same building as the list on the left.

At the base, L1, Facility. Site, power, cooling, water, safety, and physical security. This layer looks least like “AI” and does the most to set the schedule. Without power, cooling, and land, the three layers above it are a rendering.

L2, AI Infrastructure. AI compute, network, storage, the AI platform. Power becomes usable compute here. If you are short a block of cards, fabric, storage, or platform software, L3 will not run.

L3, AI Intelligence. Agents, knowledge, data, models. This is where intelligence happens. Sovereignty is often heard as “we need a model.” The page lists four things. The model is only one of them.

L4, Applications. Public services, industry copilots, research tools, learning tools. This is what the country and the public see. It cannot be bought on its own. It sits on L3, L3 sits on L2, L2 sits on L1.

The close is the engineering judgement: an AI DC needs a stable architecture that scales flexibly and keeps pace with rapid change in foundation models. Applications can be ambitious. Models can change quickly. If the architecture underneath cannot scale with them, the system dies at the bottom. So you scale the four layers as one system. You do not sign for a model and backfill the building later.

This is a vendor-neutral reference architecture for planning and procurement. Names can change with local rules. The causal chain between the layers should not. Next we turn “how big” from a slogan into a chain you can calculate.

## 09  Planning method  ·  about 3.5 minutes spoken

**On screen:** *AI DC planning methodology: Token → Card → Power*

There is one causal chain: Token → Card → Power. Size compute from the business workload. Then size power, cooling, and floor area from the cards. Each stage has a formula. You can run it forward, or reverse from megawatts or floor area. Reverse is useful. Many programmes know the power they can secure before they know the workload they can support.

Stage one. Token. Business workload. Start from users, penetration, and usage. Convert that into daily tokens, Td, and peak throughput, Ts. Td equals users times tokens per user. Ts equals Td divided by 86,400 — the seconds in a day. Watch three things: whether the product form lifts tokens per user, whether cache hit rate cuts effective throughput, and the mix of inference work. Chat, finished office work, and coding agents do not share a token curve.

Stage two. Card. Accelerator count. Convert Ts into theoretical FLOPS, then into NPU count with margin, and check the HBM memory floor. The formula takes the larger of the compute result and the memory floor. Stress the second half. The usual error in the room is to size only for FLOPS, then find the model will not fit in memory. Also watch FLOPS per token, utilization, and how you hold redundancy.

Stage three. Power. Convert cards into racks, then into IT design power, room power, and floor area. Cooling changes density. Air versus liquid is a capacity choice, not a fit-out choice. IT power is racks times kilowatts per rack. Room power is IT power times PUE. Watch cards per rack, rack kilowatts, and PUE.

The strip at the bottom is the same chain, worked through. The page says it plainly: **a worked example, not a target.** One 1,024-card liquid-cooled supernode: 40.6 billion tokens a day, 470 thousand tokens per second, 1,908 AI-active users. 1,024 cards; 128 servers times eight; 940.8 PFLOPS with margin. 2.10 MW IT design, 2.41 MW room, 206 square metres, 40 racks in two pods.

On the same method, a 768-card air-cooled node needs 2.50 MW and 404 square metres. Hold that. **More cards do not automatically mean more power or more space.** Cooling and density change the answer. So you cannot decide the card buy first and look for power and land afterwards. You have to walk the whole chain.

We have the layers and the method. The last question is when to decide, and why the programme will not wait.

## 10  The decision  ·  about 3 minutes spoken

**On screen:** *Sovereign AI needs a sovereign factory*  
**Close:** *Decide now*

The left column is The case. Four points. They are the briefing, closed.

01. Demand is already industrial. AI use and national programmes are already at that scale. Waiting is not prudence. It cedes the terms, the power, and the sites to whoever moves first.  
02. One external foundation model can become a strategic single point of dependence for knowledge, access, and control. Slide 5 becomes a reason to decide.  
03. Sovereign AI is local control of data, models, and continuity, while staying connected to global technology. Slide 6 becomes a principle.  
04. The AI data center is the foundation: the facility, the compute platform, and the Token → Card → Power plan that sizes them. Slides 7 and 8 become a place to land.

The right column is The clock. Read the Gantt for the floor, not for decoration.

AI DC facility: 12 to 18 months.  
Compute platform: 6 to 9 months.  
AI applications: 3 to 6 months.

Even if the platform and the applications run in parallel, **the facility still sets a 12-to-18-month floor under the whole programme.** An application can show a prototype in a quarter. A building will not. The ranges are indicative. Site, grid interconnection, permits, cooling, and scope will move the months. The order of magnitude is years, not weeks.

So the close is Decide now. I will read the line as written:

*Sovereign AI is not a model you rent. It is a factory you own — and the AI data center is that foundation. The building will not wait.*

## 11  Forest  ·  about 1.5 minutes spoken (silence first)

**On screen:** Five trees. Roots do not cross. The canopies become a forest.  
**Line order:** after the canopy is fully lit, the line under the roots appears first, then the line above the canopy.

**Do not speak when this slide opens.** Light travels from the roots to the crown in about four seconds. The two lines then appear in order. Wait until the upper line is standing. Leave eight to ten seconds of silence.

Each tree is a nation. The roots stay in their own ground and do not cross — that is sovereignty. Data, models, compute, and continuity need their own roots. You cannot plant them in someone else’s soil, and you cannot have none. The canopies overlap — that is a shared AI age. Nations and ecosystems can connect and grow together. Connection still requires that each tree can stand.

Point down first, then up. Read the lines as they stand.

**Every nation needs its own roots.**

**Together they become the forest of the AI age.**

The decision in front of you is to put this nation’s roots in the ground: the site, the power, the four-layer factory, and a plan you can calculate. Without roots, the canopy cannot join. With roots, a forest is possible.

That is the briefing. I am happy to take questions.

## Likely questions (not inside the 30 minutes)

1. **Are tokens power?** No. Slide 3 says token volume measures usage, not power. Power is the chain on slide 9.  
2. **Which country is spending the most?** The figures are different measures. Slide 4 already says so. Do not rank them.  
3. **Should we deploy 1,024 cards?** No. That is a worked example. The page says it is not a target.  
4. **Can we launch applications and wait on the building?** Applications take 3–6 months. The facility takes 12–18. Parallel work does not remove that floor.  
5. **Does sovereignty mean we refuse global models?** No. Slide 7 is explicit: use global technology, keep local control, and hold a fallback for critical services.
